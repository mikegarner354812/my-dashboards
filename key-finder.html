Your Two Candidates

Every key signature is shared by exactly one major key and one minor key (its relative minor) — so the signature alone always leaves you with a choice of two.

The Shortcut

How that major key was worked out from the accidentals alone — no memorizing all 15 signatures required.

03

Which One Is It?

The signature narrows it to two. This is what actually decides between the major key and its relative minor.

1

Check the last note or chord

Pieces almost always end on the tonic. If it ends on the major-key note/chord, you're in the major. If it ends on the minor-key note/chord, you're in the minor.

2

Check the first note or chord too

A good second clue — pieces often (not always) open on or near the tonic. Weight this less than the ending.

3

Look for accidentals that aren't in the signature

A sharp written in by hand on the minor key's 7th degree, over and over, is the raised leading tone of harmonic minor — a strong sign you're in the minor, not the major.

4

If you can see the chords, use those

A piece that keeps landing on the minor chord (not the major one) is in the minor key, signature notwithstanding.
